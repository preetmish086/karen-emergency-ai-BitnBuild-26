\section{Problem Definition and Operational Context}
\subsection{The Challenge: Information Deluge in Severe Urban Crises}
During catastrophic civic events (fires, structural collapses, explosions, mass-casualty incidents), municipal 911 public safety answering points (PSAPs) and digital citizen feeds experience acute operational breakdown:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Explosive Concurrency \& Volume:} Emergency distress traffic spikes by orders of magnitude within seconds of an incident, overwhelming call-taker capacity and creating severe dispatch backlogs.
  \item \textbf{Panicked Linguistic Fragmentation:} Real citizens transmit truncated, ungrammatical, and panicked messages (e.g., \emph{"5th ave fire big smoke help stuck inside"}), lacking standard dispatch taxonomies.
  \item \textbf{Redundancy \& Ambient Chatter:} Hundreds of calls report the same visible incident with conflicting details, while benign mentions (e.g., movie shoots, drills, sports fireworks) pollute the emergency stream.
  \item \textbf{Critical Detection Latency:} Dispatchers forced to manually read every unstructured message inevitably suffer cognitive fatigue, delaying life-saving deployment to trapped victims.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Official Hackathon Problem Statement}
\begin{calloutbox}{Track 2: ``Karen's Ear'' --- AI Emergency Dispatch Assistant}
\small
\textbf{The Web We Weave:} When New York is in trouble, the calls and messages pour in all at once, panicked, half-finished, overlapping. \emph{``There's smoke on 5th.''} \emph{``Someone's stuck under the rubble.''} \emph{``The lizard-thing is heading downtown.''} Buried in that flood of words is exactly the information Spider-Man needs: what's happening, where, and how urgent. But no human can read it all fast enough, and by the time the important message is spotted, it's too late. Karen, Peter's AI assistant, needs to cut through the chaos and tell him where to swing first.

\vspace{0.12cm}
\textbf{Your Mission:} Build an assistant that reads incoming emergency reports and messages, understands what each one is actually about, and decides which ones matter most right now, turning a chaotic stream of text into a clear, prioritised picture of where help is needed.
\end{calloutbox}

\subsection{Human-in-the-Loop Philosophy \& Zero-Hallucination Directives}
Karen's Ear is designed as an \textbf{intelligence amplification and decision-support layer}. It preserves dispatcher autonomy while automating the heavy cognitive burden of message ingestion, parsing, entity extraction, deduplication, and priority ranking.
To guarantee operational safety in life-or-death scenarios, the system enforces strict \textbf{Anti-Hallucination Directives}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If an incident location is not explicitly stated or geocoded, \texttt{location} strictly defaults to \texttt{null} with safe coordinate fallback; models are prohibited from guessing physical addresses.
  \item If casualty or trapped victim numbers are unstated, casualty fields strictly remain \texttt{null}; no synthetic victim counts are ever fabricated.
  \item If hazard classification cannot be established with statistical certainty, \texttt{incident\_type} evaluates to \texttt{unknown}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Real-Time Append-Only CSV Audit Engine (\texttt{raw\_emergencies.csv})}
In emergency governance and legal discovery, unalterable audit trails are mandatory. The backend incorporates a dedicated real-time streaming audit engine:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Every report ingested via the \texttt{/ingest} endpoint is instantaneously appended to \texttt{raw\_emergencies.csv}.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Decoupling Relevance Filtering from Priority Triage}
A fundamental architectural triumph of Karen's Ear is the strict decoupling of \textbf{Relevance} from \textbf{Priority}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Relevance Gate:} Answers the classification query: \emph{``Does this transmission report a genuine emergency?''}
  \item \textbf{Priority Engine:} Answers the resource allocation query: \emph{``Where must first responders deploy first?''}
\end{itemize}
Conflating these dimensions introduces catastrophic failure modes. For example, a citizen reporting \emph{"a cat is stuck in a tree in Central Park"} is $100\%$ emergency-relevant, yet possesses low priority ($\mathcal{P} \approx 0.25$). Conversely, a fragmented whisper \emph{"bomb subway station"} carries low grammatical certainty, but demands immediate maximum priority deployment ($\mathcal{P} \ge 0.90$). Decoupling guarantees that low-urgency events are preserved without preempting acute hazards, while ambiguous high-threat events are surfaced instantly.

\subsection{The 6-Phase Operational Workflow}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Detect (Intake \& Relevance Gating):} Ingests incoming distress payloads through asynchronous FastAPI endpoints, validates schemas, logs to \texttt{raw\_emergencies.csv}, and categorizes reports into \texttt{HIGH}, \texttt{UNCERTAIN}, or \texttt{LOW}.
  \item \textbf{Understand (Role 2 NLP \& Entity Extraction):} Employs domain tokenizers and pattern matching to classify incident categories, resolve named landmarks (40+ NYC coordinates), and extract casualty counts (\texttt{affected}, \texttt{injured}, \texttt{trapped}) with zero hallucination.
  \item \textbf{Corroborate (Multi-Caller Clustering):} Identifies duplicate reports referencing the identical physical event using spatiotemporal proximity and landmark matching, computing cluster size $N$.
  \item \textbf{Predict (Multi-Attribute Priority Regression):} Evaluates normalized severity ($S$), actionability ($A$), credibility ($C$), and corroboration bonus ($\Delta_k$) via formula and Random Forest models.
  \item \textbf{Rank (Active Priority Queue):} Inserts incident records into a dynamically sorted, priority-descending queue to ensure dispatchers focus on acute life-safety hazards.
  \item \textbf{Dispatch (Tactical Action \& Auditing):} Projects incidents onto the PyDeck 3D Manhattan radar HUD, sounds the P1 Spider-Sense alert for critical incidents, and empowers dispatchers to deploy response units with a single click.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Machine Learning and Algorithmic Methodology}
\subsection{Tri-State Relevance Gate \& Lexical Safety Net}
Binary classifiers in emergency dispatch suffer high false-negative rates on noisy, out-of-vocabulary citizen text. Karen's Ear deploys a \textbf{Tri-State Architecture}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{HIGH ($P \ge 0.75$):} Unambiguous, high-confidence emergency signals with actionable details.
  \item \textbf{UNCERTAIN ($0.40 \le P < 0.75$):} Fragmented, ambiguous, or borderline reports passed forward for human verification.
  \item \textbf{LOW ($P < 0.40$):} Verified benign chatter, spam, or explicit non-emergency statements.
\end{itemize}

Feature extraction uses \textbf{TF-IDF} with sublinear term frequency scaling and word unigram/bigram ranges ($n \in \{1, 2\}$):
\begin{equation}
  \text{TF-IDF}(t, d, D) = \big(1 + \log(\text{TF}(t, d))\big) \times \log\left(\frac{1 + |D|}{1 + |\{d \in D : t \in d\}|}\right) + 1
\end{equation}
The vectorizer feeds a multinomial \textbf{Logistic Regression} classifier:
\begin{equation}
  P(y = k \mid \mathbf{x}) = \frac{\exp(\mathbf{w}_k^T \phi(\mathbf{x}) + b_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \exp(\mathbf{w}_j^T \phi(\mathbf{x}) + b_j)}, \quad k \in \{\text{HIGH}, \text{UNCERTAIN}, \text{LOW}\}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Deterministic Safety Net Guard:} To prevent statistical blind spots on rare disaster keywords, the ML prediction is wrapped in a deterministic safety net. The system scans for critical trigger stems (\texttt{explosion}, \texttt{collapse}, \texttt{gunshot}, \texttt{trapped}, \texttt{bleeding}, \texttt{fire}, \texttt{rubble}) and verifies the absence of non-emergency exclusion contexts (\texttt{drill}, \texttt{movie}, \texttt{film}, \texttt{rehearsal}, \texttt{fireworks}). If a trigger is present without exclusion and the statistical model predicted \texttt{LOW}, the report is automatically escalated to \textbf{\texttt{UNCERTAIN}}. It is never silently dropped.

\subsection{Role 2: 8-Factor Multi-Signal Credibility Model}
Unlike primitive dispatch systems that assume uniform caller reliability, Karen's Ear computes an explainable, continuous credibility score $C \in [0.0, 1.0]$ synthesizing eight distinct linguistic, structural, and consensus signals (Table~\ref{tab:credibility_factors}):

The composite credibility score $C$ is computed via weighted linear aggregation:
\begin{equation}
  C = \mathrm{round}\left(\min\left(1.0, \; \max\left(0.0, \; \sum_{i=1}^{7} w_i \cdot F_i\right)\right), \; 2\right), \quad \text{where } \sum_{i=1}^{7} w_i = 1.00
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multi-Attribute Priority Engine Formulation}
The Priority Engine calculates continuous priority $\mathcal{P} \in [0.0, 1.0]$ by synthesizing normalized severity ($S$), actionability ($A$), credibility ($C$), and an additive corroboration bonus ($\Delta_k$):
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{P}_{\text{raw}} = \big(0.45 \cdot S\big) + \big(0.35 \cdot A\big) + \big(0.20 \cdot C\big) + \Delta_k
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_k = \min\Big(0.05, \; (N - 1) \times 0.015\Big), \quad \mathcal{P} = \mathrm{round}\Big(\max\left(0.0, \; \min\left(1.0, \; \mathcal{P}_{\text{raw}}\right)\right), \; 4\Big)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Ensemble Random Forest Priority Regressor}
To model non-linear interactions across severity, actionability, credibility, and caller cluster count, an ensemble \textbf{Random Forest Regressor} was trained:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\text{RF}}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^B T_b(\mathbf{x}), \quad B = 100 \text{ estimators}, \; \mathbf{x} = [S, A, C, N]
\end{equation}
The ensemble achieves exceptional ranking smoothness, resisting label noise while faithfully predicting multi-attribute urgency.

\section{Production System Implementation}
\subsection{High-Performance FastAPI Ingestion Backend (\texttt{backend.py})}
The backend is built on Python 3.11+ and FastAPI (ASGI/Starlette), delivering sub-millisecond route latency:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{POST /ingest} (and \texttt{/api/reports}): Ingests distress text, executes real-time CSV audit logging, invokes the Role 2 NLP pipeline, runs the Priority Engine, auto-resolves coordinates, and registers the incident in memory.
  \item \texttt{GET /reports} (and \texttt{/api/reports}): Returns all active incidents sorted strictly descending by priority score $\mathcal{P}$. Supports real-time filtering by \texttt{incident\_type} and \texttt{severity}.
  \item \texttt{PATCH /api/reports/\{id\}/dispatch}: Dispatches emergency apparatus (e.g., \emph{"Engine 5"}, \emph{"Web Strike"}), updating status from \texttt{pending} to \texttt{dispatched}.
  \item \texttt{GET /api/stats}: Emits real-time operational metrics: total volume, active critical incidents, and hazard breakdowns.
  \item \texttt{GET /health}: Liveness and readiness probe reporting server status and total active incident volume.
  \item \texttt{POST /api/reports/reset}: Restores initial demonstration scenarios for continuous testing and evaluation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Landmark \& Coordinate Auto-Resolution Engine}
Because panicked callers rarely know their exact GPS coordinates, \texttt{backend.py} incorporates an intelligent multi-tiered geocoding engine:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Direct Telemetry:} Inspects device \texttt{latitude}/\texttt{longitude} floats or \texttt{gps\_xy} strings.
  \item \textbf{Named Landmark Dictionary:} Maintains coordinates for 40+ New York landmarks and boroughs (Times Square, Grand Central, Central Park, Brooklyn Bridge, Wall Street, Queens Blvd, Flushing, Astoria, DUMBO, Harlem, etc.).
  \item \textbf{Natural Language Regex Extraction:} Scans the raw distress text for New York geographic markers if metadata fields are empty.

\section{Architectural Decisions and Rationale Matrix}
Every engineering decision in Karen's Ear is documented with an explicit operational justification:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Why Tri-State instead of Binary Relevance?} Binary classification fails on fragmented distress messages. The \texttt{UNCERTAIN} buffer ensures ambiguous messages containing partial emergency signals are routed for verification rather than silently discarded.
  \item \textbf{Why TF-IDF Vectorization over Heavy LLMs?} Emergency dispatch requires sub-10ms response times. TF-IDF paired with Logistic Regression is ultra-fast, deterministic, CPU-efficient, and operates reliably in edge environments without external cloud API dependencies.
  \item \textbf{Why Bigrams ($n=2$)?} Emergency distress is conveyed through short collocations (\emph{"gas leak"}, \emph{"shots fired"}, \emph{"water rising"}). Bigrams capture critical contextual urgency that unigrams miss.
  \item \textbf{Why a Context-Aware Safety Net?} Statistical models inevitably fail on rare out-of-vocabulary phrases. The safety net guarantees critical hazards (\emph{"rubble trapped"}) are never dropped due to vocabulary sparsity.
  \item \textbf{Why not force all keywords to HIGH?} Emergency terms frequently appear in benign contexts (drills, movies, fireworks). Blindly forcing \texttt{HIGH} causes severe alert fatigue. Escalating to \texttt{UNCERTAIN} balances safety with selectivity.
  \item \textbf{Why an 8-Factor Credibility Engine?} Single-score credibility is brittle. Decomposing credibility into observation, specificity, coherence, actionability, casualty signals, density consensus, and consistency provides complete explainability.
  \item \textbf{Why Streamlit + PyDeck for the Tactical HUD?} PyDeck provides high-performance 3D GPU-accelerated spatial rendering of NYC landmarks, while Streamlit enables rapid real-time state management, audio synthesis, and seamless dispatcher controls.
  \item \textbf{Why Newline-Sanitized Streaming CSV Logging?} Regulatory compliance requires immutable, auditable records. Sanitizing multiline distress text prevents CSV injection and preserves row integrity across data pipelines.
\end{itemize}

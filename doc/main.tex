\begin{calloutbox}{Executive Summary \& Core Operational Paradigm}
\small
\textbf{Karen's Ear} is an intelligent, edge-resilient emergency triage assistant designed to cut through the noise, fragmentation, and volume of civic distress communications. Built around the six-phase operational paradigm:
\begin{center}
  \textbf{\color{DarkNavy} Detect} $\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$ 
  \textbf{\color{DarkNavy} Understand} $\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$ 
  \textbf{\color{DarkNavy} Corroborate} $\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$ 
  \textbf{\color{DarkNavy} Predict} $\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$ 
  \textbf{\color{DarkNavy} Rank} $\boldsymbol{\longrightarrow}$ 
  \textbf{\color{DarkNavy} Dispatch}
\end{center}
The system pairs tri-state relevance filtering (High / Uncertain / Low) and a lexical safety net with multi-attribute priority regression ($\mathcal{P} \in [0.0, 1.0]$) to transform incoming text streams into an actionable, prioritized picture of emergency need.
\end{calloutbox}

\section{Problem Definition and Operational Context}
\subsection{The Challenge: Information Deluge in Emergency Dispatch}
In high-stress urban crises, 911 dispatch centers and digital communication feeds face catastrophic information overload:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Bursting Volume \& Concurrency:} Emergency distress traffic spikes by orders of magnitude within minutes of an incident, causing cognitive bottlenecks.
  \item \textbf{Linguistic Fragmentation:} Distress messages arrive as panicked, incomplete sentence fragments (e.g., \emph{"5th ave fire big smoke help stuck"}).
  \item \textbf{Redundancy \& Ambient Noise:} Multiple callers report the identical incident using disparate phrasing, while non-emergency chatter dilutes urgent signals.
  \item \textbf{Semantic Ambiguity:} Critical hazard markers (\emph{"something blew up"}) must be distinguished from benign mentions (drills, film shoots, fireworks).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Official Hackathon Problem Statement}
\begin{calloutbox}{Track 2: ``Karen's Ear'' --- AI Emergency Dispatch Assistant}
\small
\textbf{The Web We Weave:} When New York is in trouble, the calls and messages pour in all at once, panicked, half-finished, overlapping. \emph{``There's smoke on 5th.''} \emph{``Someone's stuck under the rubble.''} \emph{``The lizard-thing is heading downtown.''} Buried in that flood of words is exactly the information Spider-Man needs: what's happening, where, and how urgent. But no human can read it all fast enough, and by the time the important message is spotted, it's too late. Karen, Peter's AI assistant, needs to cut through the chaos and tell him where to swing first.

\vspace{0.15cm}
\textbf{Your Mission:} Build an assistant that reads incoming emergency reports and messages, understands what each one is actually about, and decides which ones matter most right now, turning a chaotic stream of text into a clear, prioritised picture of where help is needed.
\end{calloutbox}

\subsection{System Scope and Human-in-the-Loop Philosophy}
Karen's Ear functions strictly as an \textbf{intelligence amplification and decision-support layer}. It does \emph{not} replace human first-responder judgment. Rather, it structures unstructured dispatches, clusters corroborating eyewitnesses, computes explainable priority scores, and highlights critical life-safety hazards, enabling sub-second tactical triage.

\subsection{Decoupling Relevance Gate from Priority Ranking}
Relevance answers: \emph{``Does this message describe a real emergency?''} Priority answers: \emph{``Where must first responders deploy first?''} Conflating these dimensions causes dangerous operational confusion. A report saying \emph{"a cat is stuck on a garden fence"} is $100\%$ emergency-relevant, but has low priority ($\approx 0.20$). Conversely, a vague fragment \emph{"bomb subway"} carries low grammatical certainty, but demands maximum priority vigilance ($\approx 0.95$). Decoupling guarantees that low-urgency events are not discarded as noise, while ambiguous high-threat events are immediately flagged.

\subsection{The 8-Stage Operational Flow}
The Karen's Ear data pipeline executes eight disciplined transformations:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Raw Report Intake:} Accepts multi-channel distress signals via asynchronous REST endpoints, generates unique identifiers, and normalizes encodings.
  \item \textbf{Tri-State Relevance Gate:} Evaluates reports using TF-IDF and multinomial Logistic Regression into \texttt{HIGH}, \texttt{UNCERTAIN}, or \texttt{LOW}. Critical emergency markers are rescued via the lexical safety net.
  \item \textbf{Incident Extraction:} Normalizes text into standard hazard categories (\texttt{fire}, \texttt{explosion}, \texttt{collapse}, etc.), extracts landmarks, and assesses severity and actionability.
  \item \textbf{Corroboration Engine:} Groups concurrent reports referencing identical events using string distance and spatiotemporal clustering, dynamically incrementing credibility ($\mathcal{C}$) based on cluster size $N$.
  \item \textbf{Priority Engine:} Synthesizes severity, actionability, credibility, and corroboration bonuses into a continuous, clamped priority score $\mathcal{P} \in [0.0, 1.0]$.
  \item \textbf{Incident Ranking:} Maintains an active priority queue sorted strictly descending by $\mathcal{P}$ to ensure highest-urgency events dominate dispatcher visibility.
  \item \textbf{Geolocation Aggregation:} Maps incidents into spatial sectors (e.g., Midtown, Downtown) to prevent deploying redundant apparatus to adjacent city blocks.
  \item \textbf{Dispatcher Telemetry Dashboard:} Delivers real-time incident queues, spatial coordinates, Karen voice alerts, and one-click dispatch controls to operators.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Machine Learning and Algorithmic Methodology}
\subsection{Tri-State Relevance Classification}
Binary classification is unacceptably brittle in civic safety: fragmented reports are easily dropped as false negatives. Karen's Ear introduces a \textbf{Tri-State Architecture}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{HIGH:} Unambiguous emergency markers with actionable details (e.g., \emph{"Explosion at 5th Ave, people trapped"}).
  \item \textbf{UNCERTAIN:} Ambiguous, fragmented, or unverified reports containing emergency-adjacent signals. Passed forward with review flags.
  \item \textbf{LOW:} Verified benign chatter, spam, or explicit non-emergency statements (e.g., \emph{"Did you see the explosion in the movie?"}).
\end{itemize}

Feature extraction uses \textbf{TF-IDF} with unigrams and bigrams ($n \in \{1, 2\}$) paired with a multinomial \textbf{Logistic Regression} classifier:
\begin{equation}
  P(y = k \mid \mathbf{x}) = \frac{\exp(\mathbf{w}_k^T \phi(\mathbf{x}) + b_k)}{\sum_{j=1}^K \exp(\mathbf{w}_j^T \phi(\mathbf{x}) + b_j)}, \quad k \in \{\text{HIGH}, \text{UNCERTAIN}, \text{LOW}\}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Context-Aware Emergency Signal Safety Net}
Because statistical models can under-score out-of-vocabulary emergency phrasing, Karen's Ear wraps the ML layer with a deterministic \textbf{Safety Net}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Trigger Lexicon:} Scans for critical root stems: \texttt{explosion, fire, collapse, trapped, shooter, gunshot, flood, rubble, bleeding}.
  \item \textbf{Context Exclusion:} Checks for non-emergency qualifiers: \texttt{drill, movie, film, rehearsal, documentary, fireworks, museum}.
  \item \textbf{Safety Override:} If a trigger is detected without an exclusion context, and the ML model predicted \texttt{LOW}, the classification is automatically escalated to \textbf{\texttt{UNCERTAIN}}. It is never silently dropped.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Priority Engine Formulation}
The Priority Engine computes a continuous priority metric $\mathcal{P} \in [0.0, 1.0]$. The deterministic core operates on four structured, normalized inputs (Table~\ref{tab:priority_features}).

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{Priority Engine Feature Weights and Numerical Encoding}
  \label{tab:priority_features}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{llcX}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Dimension} & \textbf{Categorical / Raw Input} & \textbf{Normalized Value} & \textbf{Formula Weight} \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{Severity} ($S$) & \texttt{critical} (1.00), \texttt{high} (0.75), \texttt{medium} (0.50), \texttt{low} (0.25) & $S \in [0.25, 1.00]$ & $w_s = 0.45$ \\
    \textbf{Actionability} ($A$) & \texttt{high} (1.00), \texttt{medium} (0.60), \texttt{low} (0.20) & $A \in [0.20, 1.00]$ & $w_a = 0.35$ \\
    \textbf{Credibility} ($C$) & Signal Confidence / Clustering & $C \in [0.00, 1.00]$ & $w_c = 0.20$ \\
    \textbf{Corroboration Bonus} ($\Delta_k$) & Incident Cluster Size $N$ & $\min(0.05, (N-1) \times 0.015)$ & Additive Boost \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The synthesized priority score $\mathcal{P}$ is defined mathematically by:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{P}_{\text{raw}} = \left(0.45 \cdot S\right) + \left(0.35 \cdot A\right) + \left(0.20 \cdot C\right) + \min\Big(0.05,\, (N - 1) \times 0.015\Big)
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{P} = \mathrm{round}\Big(\max\left(0.0, \min\left(1.0, \mathcal{P}_{\text{raw}}\right)\right),\, 4\Big)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Explainability Engine and Automated Recommendations}
In critical civic infrastructure, ``black-box'' scores are hazardous. Karen's Ear generates a real-time explainability payload for every incident, decomposing the score into its constituent drivers and providing an automated operational recommendation:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Recommendation}(\mathcal{P}) = 
  \begin{cases}
    \textbf{IMMEDIATE\_DISPATCH}, & \text{if } \mathcal{P} \ge 0.80 \\
    \textbf{HIGH\_PRIORITY\_STANDBY}, & \text{if } 0.60 \le \mathcal{P} < 0.80 \\
    \textbf{MONITOR}, & \text{if } \mathcal{P} < 0.60
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Random Forest Priority Regression Baseline}
To discover non-linear feature interactions as training datasets expand, an ensemble Random Forest Regressor is trained on historical dispatch outcomes:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\text{RF}}(\mathbf{x}) = \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B T_b(\mathbf{x}), \quad B = 100 \text{ estimators over } (S, A, C, \text{Corroboration})
\end{equation}
The ensemble baseline exhibits high stability against noisy outlier labels while producing a smooth continuous ranking curve.

\section{Evaluation Strategy and Verification Framework}
\subsection{Relevance Classification Evaluation: Why Macro Metrics Matter}
In emergency dispatch datasets, class distribution is inherently skewed: routine traffic or benign chatter outnumbers structural collapses. Relying on global \textbf{Accuracy} creates a perilous illusion of performance:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Accuracy} = \frac{\text{TP} + \text{TN}}{\text{Total Reports}}
\end{equation}
A naive model predicting $100\%$ \texttt{LOW} in a dataset containing $95\%$ non-emergencies would achieve $95\%$ accuracy while missing every life-threatening event. Therefore, Karen's Ear mandatorily evaluates on \textbf{Macro-Averaged Precision, Recall, and $F_1$-Score}:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Precision}_{\text{macro}} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \frac{\text{TP}_k}{\text{TP}_k + \text{FP}_k}, \quad
  \text{Recall}_{\text{macro}} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \frac{\text{TP}_k}{\text{TP}_k + \text{FN}_k}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  F_{1,\text{macro}} = \frac{2 \times \text{Precision}_{\text{macro}} \times \text{Recall}_{\text{macro}}}{\text{Precision}_{\text{macro}} + \text{Recall}_{\text{macro}}}
\end{equation}
Evaluating across $K \in \{\text{HIGH}, \text{UNCERTAIN}, \text{LOW}\}$ guarantees that minority life-safety classes receive equal evaluation weight.

\subsection{Priority Regression Metrics}
Continuous priority regression is evaluated across three complementary metrics:
\begin{equation}
  \text{MAE} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left| y_i - \hat{y}_i \right|, \quad
  \text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left( y_i - \hat{y}_i \right)^2}, \quad
  R^2 = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \bar{y})^2}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Automated Unit Testing \& Validation Suite}
The codebase is validated via comprehensive automated unit tests in \texttt{tests/test\_priority.py} and \texttt{tests/test\_api.py}. Tests verify that: (1) Critical severity incidents with high actionability achieve $\mathcal{P} \ge 0.90$; (2) Multiple corroborating callers boost priority dynamically; (3) Scores strictly clamp within $[0.0, 1.0]$; and (4) FastAPI endpoints maintain 100\% schema compliance.

\section{Architectural Decisions and Rationale Matrix}
Every engineering decision in Karen's Ear is documented with an explicit operational justification:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Why three relevance levels?} A binary gate is brittle. Fragmented reports that contain genuine emergency signals but lack sufficient context would be discarded. \texttt{UNCERTAIN} provides a safe review buffer.
  \item \textbf{Why TF-IDF Vectorization?} TF-IDF is lightweight, CPU-efficient, deterministic, provides sub-millisecond inference latencies, and operates without expensive GPU infrastructure.
  \item \textbf{Why Bigrams ($n=2$)?} Emergency distress is expressed through short collocations rather than single words (e.g., \emph{"shots fired"}, \emph{"water rising"}). Bigrams capture imperative intent.
  \item \textbf{Why an Emergency Safety Net?} Statistical models fail on out-of-distribution wording. The lexical safety layer guarantees critical terms (\emph{"something blew up"}) are never dropped due to vocabulary sparsity.
  \item \textbf{Why not force all keywords to HIGH?} High-consequence words appear in benign contexts (drills, movies, news). Blindly forcing \texttt{HIGH} causes alert fatigue. The safety net elevates to \texttt{UNCERTAIN}.
  \item \textbf{Why Random Forest for Priority?} The priority feature space is compact ($\le 6$ dimensions) with non-linear interactions. Random Forest models non-linear thresholds cleanly without overfitting.
  \item \textbf{Why decouple Relevance from Priority?} Relevance addresses evidentiary signal presence; Priority orders incident urgency. Conflating them prevents independent auditing of classification versus dispatch order.
  \item \textbf{Why not use raw Location as Priority?} A prominent landmark (e.g., Times Square) must not receive an artificial urgency boost over a fatal residential accident in Queens. Location is reserved for spatial grouping and routing.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dataset Generalization:} Models were trained on representative synthetic vectors; calibration on municipal dispatch logs is pending.
  \item \textbf{Keyword Lexicon:} The safety net uses curated keyword lists requiring ongoing expansion for localized dialect idioms and multilingual slang.
